## Project Description

The objective of this paper is to evaluate the causal effect of minimum wage increases on youth employment outcomes in European countries using a Difference-in-Differences (DiD) framework.

The analysis focuses on employment rates among individuals aged 15–24 and compares countries that implemented substantial minimum wage increases with countries where minimum wage policies remained relatively stable during the sample period.

The project applies standard causal inference methods commonly used in policy evaluation research, including:

* Difference-in-Differences estimation
* Fixed effects models
* Event-study analysis
* Robustness checks
* Parallel trends evaluation

The paper also discusses the institutional background of minimum wage policies in Europe and reviews the relevant labor economics literature.
